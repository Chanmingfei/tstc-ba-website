Freshman Guide (10) – Library & Study Rooms

The library and study rooms are the most-used study spaces for new students. This guide pulls together opening hours for both campuses, where the study rooms are, seat-booking and seat-saving rules, and seat-picking tips in one place—pair it with the Campus Card and Public Bathroom guides to quickly find your own study corner.

Everything below comes from current students' first-hand experience; exact times follow the on-site notices and the "Perfect Campus" app.

I. Library Opening Hours

Both campus libraries admit by swiping your campus card—no extra booking needed. Hours are extended during exam weeks, making them the top choice for cramming.

1. Daxuedao Campus Library

Sitting on the central axis near the South Campus, it is the largest branch with the most seats. Weekday hours are long; weekends are slightly shorter.

2. Xueyuanlu Campus Library

Older building with a smaller floor area, but close to the dorms and quiet—great for daily reading and homework.

3. Opening Hours at a Glance

Campus Weekdays Weekends Exam weeks
Daxuedao 7:30–22:30 8:00–22:00 until 23:00
Xueyuanlu 8:00–22:00 8:30–21:30 until 22:30

Note: Hours for holidays and winter/summer breaks are announced separately on-site.

Tip: Entry stops 15 minutes before closing and a leave reminder plays—take your belongings. Seats fill up fast in exam weeks, so arrive early.

II. Where the Study Rooms Are & How to Pick

Besides the library, teaching buildings open several study rooms each evening. Picking the right building by its character noticeably boosts focus.

1. Study Area Inside the Library

Fixed desks, good lighting, high outlet coverage—the top pick for long exam prep. The discussion zone suits group study but keep it low-volume.

2. Evening Study Rooms in Teaching Buildings

  • Liberal-arts building: lecture-hall conversions with plenty of outlets and open space—good if you need to plug in a laptop;
  • Science building: quiet but limited small rooms—great for focused problem sets and reading;
  • North / South campus classrooms: free when unscheduled; just avoid rooms that have a class.

3. Silent Pods in Building 6, South Campus

Newly installed individual silent pods—soundproof, with power—ideal for vocab drills, online interviews, or recording speaking practice. Limited in number; first come, first served.

Tip: Science-building small rooms fill up fast—arrive half an hour early. During exam crunch, book a partitioned silent seat first.

III. Booking & Seat-Saving Rules

Exam and civil-service prep seats are booked via the "Perfect Campus" mini-program; the system auto-releases seats by rule, keeping things fair and avoiding waste.

Booking Channel & Release Time

  • Tool: the "Perfect Campus" WeChat mini-program (after binding your campus card);
  • Release time: next day's seats drop at 22:00 nightly;
  • Scope: fixed seats in the library study area and some teaching-building study rooms.

Full Booking Flow

  1. Open the "Perfect Campus" mini-program and tap "Seat Booking";
  2. Pick campus, venue (library / study room) and date;
  3. Tap an available seat on the floor plan and confirm;
  4. Swipe your campus card to check in on arrival; tap "Temporary away" or "Release" in the app when you leave.

Seat-Saving Rules:

  • If you step away for more than 30 minutes, the system auto-releases the seat—no manual action needed;
  • Don't save seats with books or water bottles for long stretches—it violates the rules and takes spots from others;
  • Always "Release" when leaving the library, or you may get a violation record that blocks future bookings.

IV. Seat-Picking & Study Efficiency Tips

  • Fixed time, fixed seat: make the same slot and same seat a daily habit—your brain gets into flow faster;
  • Avoid distractions: stay away from doors, water dispensers and aisles; prefer natural light by the window;
  • Bring the gear: earplugs, a power bank and a water bottle save you from breaking rhythm with trips back and forth;
  • Off-peak study: right after dinner and during exam weeks are peaks—shift earlier or pick a quieter teaching-building room.
A good study spot isn't "grabbed"—it's "cultivated": fixed time, fixed seat beats hunting for one on the spot.
